# Does posting time matter for YouTube Shorts? (official statements, data studies, time zones for a UTC+7 creator)

Research date: 2026-11-07. Source types are labeled throughout: **[OFFICIAL]** = YouTube Help or a YouTube employee. **[STUDY]** = vendor dataset with a stated sample. **[ANECDOTE/OPINION]** = individual creator or blog claim without a dataset.

## Q1. What do YouTube officials say about upload timing for Shorts? Does time matter to the algorithm?

### Takeaway
YouTube's official line is consistent across 2023–2025: publish time is "not known to impact a video's long-term performance". The Shorts product lead said time of day is "not something that we think about optimizing for". Timing can only help *early* viewership, by putting the video in front of an already-active audience. The one recognized exception is time-sensitive content such as news.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help, "YouTube performance FAQ & Troubleshooting", item "When is the best time to publish videos?": "Publish time is not known to impact a video's long-term performance." It adds: "Publishing videos when your audience is most active can be beneficial for early viewership." — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** The same FAQ (per a secondary summary of it) says publish time *is* important for Live and Premiere formats. — [YouTube Help 141805 via search summary](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Todd Sherman, YouTube Shorts product lead, on Creator Insider (2023-08-23), about time of day: "It's not something that we think about optimizing for." — [Creator Insider video, t=327s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=327s), quoted with timestamp by [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[OFFICIAL, secondary report]** Benzinga's 2023 coverage of the same interview: Sherman said Shorts does not weight particular hours. Freshness can matter for news creators, but that is about responding quickly, not about time of day. — [Benzinga, Aug 2023](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance) (the page failed to load directly; content comes from a search summary)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Sherman, same interview: "The audience is the algorithm." — [Creator Insider, t=25s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=25s)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Rene Ritchie, YouTube Creator Liaison (video covered 2025-11-12): "The recommendation system is largely based on audience behavior." "...until your video goes live, not unlisted, not private, but actually public, there's no audience behavior data." "Waiting 24 to 48 hours is no different than waiting 24 to 48 seconds or weeks or months." He said uploading early only makes sense so copyright and monetization checks can finish first. — [Search Engine Journal, 2025-11-12](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-debunks-24-48-hour-upload-delay-recommendation/560613/); original video: [YouTube Zf7tOh6y39o](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zf7tOh6y39o)
- **[OFFICIAL, secondary report, unverified]** Secondary blogs say Ritchie posted on X (April, year unclear) that what matters to YouTube is what matters to the audience, with breaking news as the main case where timing matters. — [Mubert blog](https://mubert.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube); [Postey](https://postey.ai/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube-shorts-vs-long-form). I did not see the original X post.
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help, "Understand your YouTube audience": the "When your viewers are on YouTube" report "shows you when your viewers are online across YouTube in the last 28 days." YouTube suggests using it to "schedule a Premiere, or to plan your next live stream." The report counts viewers of "any form of your channel's content... Shorts, Video On Demand, Lives". The page does not state which time zone the report uses. It also makes no claim that publish time affects performance. — [YouTube Help 9314416](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416)

### Inferences
- YouTube's officials do not claim timing is a ranking signal. What timing *can* change is who is online when the Short first enters the feed. That affects the size and quality of the first test audience, not long-term reach.
- YouTube itself recommends the audience-activity report mainly for Premieres and Lives, the formats where publish time matters. It does not recommend it for Shorts.
- The Sherman statement is from August 2023, three years old. I found no newer official statement that contradicts it.

### Gaps
- I found no statement from Todd Beaupré (YouTube Growth & Discovery) specifically about Shorts upload time.
- I did not find a primary transcript or link for the reported 2026 Ritchie quote "recency weighs more for your short form video". It appeared only in a search summary of secondary blogs, so treat it as unverified.
- The YouTube Help page does not say which time zone the "When your viewers are on YouTube" chart uses. Third-party guides commonly say it shows the creator's local time, but I did not confirm this from an official source.

## Q2. Do Shorts get distributed over days or weeks (long tail)? Is "the first hour/24 hours decides everything" a myth?

### Takeaway
YouTube describes early Shorts views as an exploratory test of an audience, after which a Short "may get a lot more traffic or it may kind of taper off". YouTube Help also says old videos commonly take off later. I found no official YouTube statement that names "the first 24 hours" as decisive, and no official statement giving a specific long-tail duration for Shorts.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Sherman (2023-08-23) described the process as finding "a set of people that may enjoy your video"; after that, "it may get a lot more traffic or it may kind of taper off". — [Creator Insider, t=388s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=388s) via [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Sherman, on a Short that underperforms: "If that didn't go how you wanted to go, learn from it and move forward and try again." He also discouraged re-posting Shorts that didn't gain traction. — [Creator Insider, t=458s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=458s); [Benzinga 2023](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance)
- **[OFFICIAL, secondary attribution]** Ritchie reportedly described early Shorts views as "more like impression than traditional views", and Sherman called a new creator's early views "exploratory". — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/) (no timestamp given for these two quotes)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help FAQ, item "An old video recently took off, why?": "It's common for viewers to start showing more interest in old videos." This applies to all videos, not only Shorts. — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** Same FAQ: "Publishing videos when your audience is most active can be beneficial for early viewership", but publish time "is not known to impact a video's long-term performance". — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[STUDY vendor claim]** SocialPilot says Shorts "can gain traction days or weeks after posting", so consistency matters more than exact timing. It gives no data behind this claim. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)
- **[ANECDOTE/OPINION]** A third-party blog says a Short can keep gaining views for 3–6 weeks. The same blog calls its numbers starting points, not confirmed by the platform. — [Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/youtube-shorts-algorithm-2026)
- **[ANECDOTE/OPINION]** Creator post on X: upload time "can help the initial burst", but views "average out" after days or weeks; "This matters way less for Shorts though. They get shoved into feeds regardless". — [Muaaz on X](https://x.com/mws/status/2042019547385917719)

### Inferences
- In YouTube's own description, the first test audience is the gate, not a fixed clock window. A Short that fails its first test can still be shown again later, but YouTube gives no guarantee.
- The "first 24 hours" framing comes from vendors and blogs (for example vidIQ figures about "first 24 hours" views, see Q3), not from YouTube.

### Gaps
- I found no official YouTube data on what share of a typical Short's lifetime views arrives in the first day or week.
- I found no official statement from Todd Beaupré or Creator Insider dated 2025–2026 specifically about the Shorts long tail.

## Q3. What do large-scale studies say about the best days and hours for Shorts?

### Takeaway
The vendor studies disagree, and some contradict themselves between years. Buffer's July 2026 study (1.8M videos) is the largest Shorts-specific one. It favors Friday, then Saturday and Thursday, with evenings around 6–11 PM *local time* and a single best slot of Friday 4 PM. SocialPilot (301K videos) favors 12–2 PM or 6–7 PM local, with Friday 4–7 PM strongest. Adobe (24,274 Shorts, 2025) picks Saturday 4 PM for views. None of them clearly defines whose "local time" they mean, and none controls for content quality.

### Cited Findings

**Buffer, 2026 (most recent, largest, Shorts-specific)** **[STUDY]**
- Published 2026-07-24, author Kirsti Lang. Dataset: "1.8 million videos" (the intro says "almost 2 million"), both Shorts and long-form. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Metric: "median engagement", reported as a unitless "engagement factor". Buffer does not define how engagement is calculated. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Time zone: times were adjusted so that "the recommended times apply in your local time". Buffer does not say whether this is the uploader's or the viewer's local time. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Shorts best slot: Friday 4 PM, then Friday 6 PM and Friday 7 PM. Day ranking: Friday > Saturday > Thursday. Worst days: Tuesday and Monday. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Shorts pattern: evenings dominate, "between 6 p.m. and 11 p.m." in one section and "6 p.m.–9 p.m." in another. Afternoons 12–5 PM are weakest, except on Friday. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Shorts top 3 slots by day: Mon 8 PM, 5 PM, 6 PM. Tue 8 PM, 9 PM, 7 PM. Wed 7 PM, 8 PM, 9 PM. Thu 7 PM, 8 PM, 9 PM. Fri 4 PM, 6 PM, 7 PM. Sat 7 PM, 11 AM, 6 PM. Sun 7 PM, 8 PM, 5 PM. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Long-form, for contrast: best slot Sunday 10 AM (engagement factor 0.95). Mornings 8–11 AM perform best. Thursday 2 PM scored 0.11. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- Instability: Buffer says its 2025 analysis pointed to weekday 3–5 PM, so the patterns "have shifted". — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- An older Buffer article (about 2025) said the best time for Shorts "and all YouTube content" was Wednesday 4 PM, then Thursday and Monday 4 PM. That dataset used median views of *all* YouTube videos, not Shorts alone. **Outdated; superseded by the 2026 study.** — [Buffer, How to schedule YouTube Shorts](https://buffer.com/resources/how-to-schedule-youtube-shorts/)
- Buffer's own caveats: these are "general recommendations"; posting time is "only one factor"; creators should check YouTube Studio's "When your viewers are on YouTube" report (last 28 days). — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)

**SocialPilot (date not stated on page)** **[STUDY]**
- Sample: "301k YouTube videos published through SocialPilot by 27k+ YouTube channels". Metric: engagement rate normalized per view. Times are "local time", but the page does not define which time zone. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)
- Shorts: best upload times 12–2 PM or 6–7 PM local. Strongest window: Friday 4–7 PM. Best days: Friday, Saturday, Thursday. Worst: 3–7 AM and Sunday evenings. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)
- All YouTube: best 2–4 PM local on weekdays, Wednesday–Friday strongest. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)
- Internal inconsistency: SocialPilot lists 3–7 AM as the worst Shorts time but elsewhere recommends 7–9 AM for Friday Shorts. Its "20–40% first-24-hour lift" figure is attributed to an external source with no published method. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/insights/best-time-to-post-on-youtube)

**Adobe Express, 2025** **[STUDY, small]**
- Published 2025-07-01. Sample: 24,274 Shorts from 280 creators in 12 categories. Data collected 2025-05-22 through the YouTube API and Social Blade. No time zone stated. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/best-times-to-post-youtube-shorts)
- Most *common* upload slot: Thursday 8 AM. Best slot for views and likes: Saturday 4 PM. Best hour: 4 PM. Best day for average views: Tuesday, but Wednesday led in the per-niche analysis (self-contradiction). — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/best-times-to-post-youtube-shorts)
- Shorts posted *off-peak* averaged 8.7M views versus 6.2M for the 8–10 AM peak window. — [Adobe Express](https://www.adobe.com/express/learn/blog/best-times-to-post-youtube-shorts)
- Weakness: 280 creators is a small and probably large-channel-biased sample (average views in the millions). Upload time is also confounded with creator identity.

**SocialChamp (2026)** **[STUDY, unverified]**
- A search summary reports about 2M videos, with Wednesday 4 PM as the top "global" slot. I did not open the page, and it is not clear whether the result is Shorts-specific. — [SocialChamp](https://www.socialchamp.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)

**vidIQ** **[secondhand only, unverified]**
- Blogs attribute two conflicting vidIQ claims. (1) Shorts posted 7–9 AM, 12–2 PM, and 6–8 PM get 30–40% more watch time than off-peak posts. (2) Afternoon and evening posts get 40–60% more views in the first 24 hours. I found no primary vidIQ study page for either claim. — [Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/best-time-to-post-youtube-shorts); [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/best-times-to-post-youtube-shorts/)

**Sprout Social, Hootsuite** **[no YouTube Shorts data found]**
- One guide reports that Sprout Social's 2026 best-times report has no YouTube window. — [Postsider](https://postsider.com/blog/best-time-2026/)
- Hootsuite's page describes a tool that uses *your own* last 30 days of results to pick posting times. I found no fixed Hootsuite study for YouTube Shorts. — [Hootsuite](https://hootsuite.com/platform/best-time-to-post-on-social-media)

### Inferences
- The studies agree loosely on late week (Thursday–Saturday) and late afternoon to evening in the *viewer's* day. They disagree on almost every specific slot.
- All vendor studies are correlational. Upload time is confounded with channel size, niche, and content quality. None isolates the effect of time on an identical video.
- Buffer's own year-over-year shift (weekday 3–5 PM in 2025 to Friday-and-evenings in 2026) suggests the "best slot" signal is weak and noisy.
- Adobe's finding that off-peak Shorts outperformed peak-hour Shorts fits the idea that crowded slots carry more competition.

### Gaps
- I found no primary vidIQ, TubeBuddy, Later, or HubSpot study on Shorts posting time with a stated sample size. These vendors were not covered by a verifiable primary source in this research.
- No study published its effect size in a way that compares the best and worst slot for views (for example "the best slot gets X% more views than the median").
- No study clearly states whether "local time" means the uploader's time zone or the audience's.

## Q4. Does posting frequency or consistency affect Shorts reach, according to YouTube?

### Takeaway
YouTube says no: its systems have no daily-upload threshold, view growth is not correlated with time between uploads, and breaks do not correlate with view changes. YouTube does list "uploading less frequently than usual" as one possible reason for less Home/Suggested traffic. That is a viewer-side effect, not a penalty.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** Sherman (2023-08-23): "There is nothing in our systems that's like when you hit the five video or 10 short threshold per day..." — [Creator Insider, t=345s](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n3jsYK_-aRU&t=345s) via [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)
- **[OFFICIAL, secondary report]** Benzinga's headline on the same interview: posting 10 videos a day doesn't affect your performance; the algorithms don't reward uploading more often. — [Benzinga, Aug 2023](https://www.benzinga.com/news/23/08/34020532/youtube-shorts-chief-debunks-popular-myths-no-posting-10-videos-a-day-doesnt-affect-your-performance)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help FAQ, "Do I need to upload daily or at least once a week?": No. YouTube "found that growth in views across uploads is not correlated with time between uploads." — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help FAQ, on taking a break: "We studied thousands of channels that took a break and found no correlation between break length and changes in views." — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help FAQ, "Why is my channel getting less traffic from Home or Suggested?": one listed reason is "Uploading less frequently than usual." — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)
- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube Help FAQ: "What can lead to a decline in overall channel views is when viewers stop watching most of your videos." — [YouTube Help 141805](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/141805?hl=en)

### Inferences
- For a channel like Life Cycle Lab, more uploads mean more chances to find a hit, not a better ranking for each upload. That is a "more lottery tickets" effect, not an algorithm reward.

### Gaps
- The YouTube Help FAQ answers apply to all videos. I found no Shorts-only frequency data from YouTube.

## Q5. How should a creator in UTC+7 with a global English audience pick a posting time? Conversions to UTC+7.

### Takeaway
Use the channel's own data first. YouTube Studio > Analytics > Audience > "When your viewers are on YouTube" shows when *this channel's* viewers are online (last 28 days). Use "Top geographies" to see which countries actually watch. The vendor "4 PM / 6–11 PM local" times only help after converting them to the viewer's region. For US evenings, that means early morning to midday in Vietnam.

### Cited Findings
- **[OFFICIAL]** The "When your viewers are on YouTube" report "shows you when your viewers are online across YouTube in the last 28 days", including Shorts viewers. — [YouTube Help 9314416](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314416)
- **[STUDY]** Buffer recommends the same report for a channel's own best time. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-youtube/)
- **[ANECDOTE/OPINION]** Third-party guides say the report may show no data if a channel lacks enough traffic in the period. — [Creator Essentials glossary](https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/when-your-viewers-are-on-youtube/); [ITGeared](https://www.itgeared.com/when-your-viewers-are-on-youtube/)
- **[ANECDOTE/OPINION]** For international audiences, some guides suggest posting 2–4 PM ET for visibility across time zones, with no data disclosed. — [Miracamp](https://www.miracamp.com/learn/youtube/what-is-the-best-time-to-post)

### Time zone conversion table (computed, no source needed beyond standard offsets)
Offsets: Vietnam = UTC+7, no daylight saving time (DST). US DST ended 2026-11-01, so from November to early March, ET = UTC−5 (+12 h to Vietnam) and PT = UTC−8 (+15 h). During US DST (March to early November), ET = UTC−4 (+11 h) and PT = UTC−7 (+14 h). The UK uses GMT = UTC+0 (+7 h) in winter and BST = UTC+1 (+6 h) in summer; it left BST on 2026-10-25. India = UTC+5:30 (+1:30 h). Philippines/Malaysia/Singapore = UTC+8 (−1 h).

| Recommendation (viewer local) | US East (ET), winter → Vietnam | US West (PT), winter → Vietnam | UK (GMT) → Vietnam | India (IST) → Vietnam |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Buffer top Shorts slot: Fri 4 PM | Sat 04:00 | Sat 07:00 | Fri 23:00 | Fri 17:30 |
| Buffer Shorts evening 6–11 PM | next day 06:00–11:00 | next day 09:00–14:00 | 01:00–06:00 next day | 19:30–00:30 |
| SocialPilot Shorts 12–2 PM | 00:00–02:00 next day | 03:00–05:00 next day | 19:00–21:00 | 13:30–15:30 |
| Adobe Sat 4 PM (views) | Sun 04:00 | Sun 07:00 | Sat 23:00 | Sat 17:30 |
| Miracamp 2–4 PM ET | 02:00–04:00 next day | — | — | — |

During US DST, subtract 1 hour from the ET and PT columns. For example, Buffer's Friday 4 PM ET becomes Saturday 03:00 in Vietnam.

### The user's own posts, converted (computed)
- Giant freshwater prawn, Saturday 2026-10-31 at 21:31 Vietnam (~1M views): **Saturday 10:31 ET** (US was still on DST), Saturday 07:31 PT, Saturday 14:31 UK (already on GMT), Saturday 20:01 India. Buffer lists Saturday 11 AM (local) as a top-3 Saturday Shorts slot, close to 10:31 ET. This is a coincidence, not a cause.
- Rice field crab, the same Saturday at 11:30 Vietnam (~54K views): **Saturday 00:30 ET**, Friday 21:30 PT, Saturday 04:30 UK, Saturday 10:00 India.
- Later Shorts at 21:00–21:45 Vietnam (400–900 views): now that US DST has ended, these posts land at about 09:00–09:45 ET, 06:00–06:45 PT, 14:00–14:45 UK, and 19:30–20:15 India.

### Inferences
- The same 21:00–21:45 Vietnam slot produced ~1M views once and 400–900 views several times. A spread of more than 1,000× within one time slot means posting time cannot be the main driver here. The content, hook, and first-test-audience response explain more, which fits YouTube's official position (Q1, Q2).
- For an English-language audience, US and UK evenings fall in Vietnam's early morning (US: about 06:00–14:00 Vietnam next day; UK: about 01:00–06:00 Vietnam). If the channel wants a US-evening slot, the user must schedule the upload in YouTube Studio instead of posting live at night.
- If Analytics shows the audience is mostly South or Southeast Asia, Vietnam evening times (about 18:00–23:00) already match local evenings there.
- A practical rule consistent with the sources: (1) read "Top geographies" and "When your viewers are on YouTube" in Studio; (2) schedule 0–2 hours before the darkest band in that chart; (3) keep one slot fixed for several uploads so it does not become a variable; (4) judge results over weeks, since YouTube says early timing affects only early viewership.
- New or small channels may not have enough data for the audience-activity report. In that case, a US-evening-based default is the most defensible starting guess. In winter, that means about 05:00–10:00 Vietnam on Friday–Sunday mornings, which are Thursday–Saturday evenings in ET. This default comes from vendor data and is still a weak signal.

### Gaps
- The channel's real audience geography is unknown. All conversion advice depends on it.
- No study reports *where* Shorts viewers are when the uploader is in Asia. Buffer's "your local time" is ambiguous on this point.
- I found no official YouTube statement on whether Shorts are first tested with audiences in the uploader's country or language region.
