## Bài viết

Up close with the desert locust (Schistocerca gregaria) 🦗

This is the adult, the final stage of its life cycle, and the one that can turn into a swarm. A few details you only see this close:

👁️ Striped eyes. Each vertical band on the compound eye was laid down at a moult. The stripes are a record of how many times it has shed its skin.

📡 Antennae and palps. The long thread-like antennae smell the air. The small palps around the mouth taste a leaf before the dark-tipped mandibles cut into it.

🦵 Spring-loaded legs. The huge hind femur with the chevron pattern stores energy like a bow. One kick launches it almost a metre, and the two rows of spines on the tibia grip whatever it lands on.

🪽 Spotted wings. The narrow forewings reach past the tip of the abdomen. On a good wind an adult can travel 100 km or more in a day.

🎨 Two colours, one species. Alone, it stays sandy brown and hides. In a crowd it changes: young adults turn pink, then mature into bright yellow. That colour switch is the sign a swarm is forming. A single swarm of 1 km² can hold tens of millions of locusts, each eating its own weight in food every day.
